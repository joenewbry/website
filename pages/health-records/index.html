1

Make the two folders

On a computer, open Google Drive. Check the account picture at the top right to make sure you’re in the account you want to use.

In My Drive, click New → New folder. Enter the first name below and click Create. Repeat for the second folder.

📁 Paul Health Records

Start directly in My Drive, outside any folder already shared with others. If you prefer separate Google accounts, each person can make their own folder in their own account.

2

Check who can open each folder

Right-click a folder and choose Share. Keep General access → Restricted. Check the people listed, and repeat for the other folder.

If you want someone to help, add their specific email address. Choose Viewer to let them read, or Editor if they need to add and organize files. Editors can also change or delete files. Files inside a shared folder inherit its access.

Keep health records off “Anyone with the link.” Each person should use their own login; you can share folders without sharing passwords.

3

Put one record in the right folder

Download a record from your doctor’s patient portal, preferably as a PDF (a document that keeps its layout). Look in your computer’s Downloads folder. For paper records, use a clear scan or photo with every page included.

Open the correct person’s folder in Drive. Choose New → File upload, select the saved document, and wait for the upload to finish.

Open the uploaded file. Check the patient name, document date and all pages. Keep the original record. If Drive says storage is full, stop and resolve that before assuming the file has been saved.

4

Name it so you can find it

Right-click the file → Rename. Try date — clinic — document type, using the date on the record.

Invented example
2026-10-01 — Clinic — Visit summary.pdf

Find it later with the search box in Drive, or open the person’s folder. Start with these two folders; you can add folders for years or document types when you need them.

5

Optional: ask ChatGPT or Claude for help

Keep your originals in Drive. An artificial intelligence (AI) assistant can help explain a document or prepare questions for a doctor.

Before uploading: review the service’s current privacy and training settings. Uploading sends a copy to that service; restricted Drive sharing does not control that copy. Only share what you’re comfortable sharing. You can ask general questions without attaching records.

In Drive, right-click one document → Download. Open a new chat in ChatGPT or Claude. Use the attachment control to choose the saved file. In Claude, choose + → Add files or photos. Wait until the file is attached, then paste the prompt below and send it. Controls and limits may vary by account.

Use a separate chat for each person. Pasting a restricted Drive link may not give the assistant access. This download-and-attach method does not connect or automatically sync your Drive.

A prompt you can copy

Summarize only the attached document in plain English. Explain unfamiliar terms. For each important fact, give the page number or quote the relevant short passage. List questions I can ask my doctor. Say when information is missing or unreadable. Do not guess, diagnose, or recommend medication changes. Keep this person’s information separate from anyone else’s.

6

Check the answer and keep the original

Compare important facts with the source document, especially names, dates, numbers and medications. If the assistant cannot read a page, use a clearer copy or ask your clinician.

Use the answer to prepare questions for your clinician. Do not change treatment based on an AI answer. If you save a summary in Drive, label it AI draft — needs review and keep it alongside the original.

After the next appointment, repeat: download, upload, open and check.
