Epic ultra Oyster Bay run

E-bike up the Blanchard Mountain roads from Lake Samish, then run the Pacific Northwest Trail to Oyster Dome.

Thanksgiving week 2026 · Planning draft, November 20, 2026 · Not yet confirmed on the ground

Short answer on the e-bike question. The e-bikes can very likely get you to Samish Overlook, about 4.5 miles up gravel road from Barrel Springs Road. I could not confirm an e-bike path all the way to the dome, and the rules suggest there isn’t one. Washington DNR says Class 1, 2 and 3 e-bikes are allowed only on motorized trails and forest roads open to motorized public use. The Oyster Dome trail is a hiking trail (part of the Pacific Northwest Trail), so the last stretch is on foot. That means Chad can come on the bike leg, then run or hike with you.

I also found no source that mentions a power-line road or any trail from Lake Samish itself to Blanchard Mountain. If the family knows one, send me the name or a GPS track and I’ll add it.

Totals from the Barrel Springs turn. Bikes: about 4.5 miles each way. On foot: about 5 miles round trip, with roughly 1,050 feet of climbing and a high point of 2,025 feet. Add the Lily Lake loop (WTA lists 5.5 miles and 1,360 feet from the overlook) if you want it to be an ultra-style day. Add your own mileage from the Lake Samish house to the turn.

Plan for the day

TimeWhat
8:30Leave the house. Batteries full, tires checked.
9:15Reach the gate. Check it is open (see below).
10:00Samish Overlook. Lock the bikes, change into trail shoes, start running.
11:00Oyster Dome. Eat, take photos, add layers.
12:00Optional: Lily Lake and Max’s Shortcut loop back to the overlook.
1:30Back at the bikes. Ride down.
2:30Home, well before dark.

Late November in Bellingham has roughly 8 hours of daylight (sunset around 4:25 p.m., to be verified for the day). The overlook gate is open from one hour before sunrise to one hour after sunset, so a late finish could lock your car out, but bikes can still go around it. Set a hard turnaround time of 1:00 p.m.

Gear

  • E-bikes with full batteries, and a spare battery or charger plan. Cold cuts range.
  • Bike lock, helmets, lights, a pump and a patch kit. The dirt road is rough.
  • Trail running shoes with grip, for wet roots and rock. Spikes are not needed unless it freezes.
  • Waterproof shell, warm hat, gloves, and a dry layer to carry. The dome is exposed and windy.
  • Headlamp for each person, whistle, and a charged phone with an offline map downloaded.
  • Food and 1.5 liters of water each. There is no water at the overlook, only vault toilets.
  • Trekking poles if the descent is slick.

Things to confirm before Thanksgiving

  • E-bikes on the access road. Ask DNR (Northwest Region) whether the Blanchard roads are open to motor vehicles and e-bikes. DNR says it is still assessing e-bike rules.
  • Discover Pass. Required at Samish Overlook for vehicles. I did not find whether riders arriving by bike need one.
  • Road condition. The WTA calls the access a “bit of a rough road.” Check recent trip reports for snow, washouts and the gate.
  • Lake Samish connection. Check the DNR Blanchard trail map for any link from the lake side.
  • Trail details. The two WTA pages disagree on the junction distance (1.5 versus 1.9 miles), so trust a GPS track over either.
